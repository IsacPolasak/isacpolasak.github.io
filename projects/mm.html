Magical Mates

A magical companion for stories, routines, and voice notes.

Product: Magical Mates is a smart stuffed animal and app that helps children build routines and spark imagination. Parents use the app to send voice messages and stories, which are delivered through the plush toy using AI-generated audio.

Problem: Parents want to reduce screen time while staying connected and supporting routines. Most tools lack emotional warmth or imaginative play.

Goal: Create a magical, screen-free experience that helps families stay connected, build routines, and spark imagination through smart, voice-enabled plush toys.

Tools Figma, Adobe, Photopea

Timeline 3 weeks

Role Research, Branding, UX/UI, Prototyping, Usability-testing

Understanding The Problem and the User

Before conducting research

I assumed parents wanted their kids to have a screen-free way to stay connected with their parents and make routines easier.

Through user interviews and observation

I confirmed many struggled to balance busy schedules with quality bonding moments. A mom of a five-year-old shared how hard it was to maintain a consistent bedtime routine when she worked late shifts. She wanted a comforting, familiar presence her child could rely on when she wasn't there.

In speaking with parents and children

I discovered emotional connection was as important as structure. Parents valued tools that helped with routines, and children responded best to interactions that felt personal with a dash of magic.

User quotes

— Mom of five-year-old
"My son listens so much better when it's coming from a character he loves. If it feels like play, it doesn't feel like a routine."

Pain Points

Screen Time: Parents want to reduce their children's screen time but struggle to find engaging, screen-free alternatives that still foster connection.

Impersonality: Existing routine and storytelling tools feel impersonal and lack emotional warmth or imaginative play.

Boredom: Kids lose interest in static toys or apps that don't feel "alive" or personalized to them.

Key Takeaways

Emotional Connection: Parents prioritize maintaining intimate bonds with their children despite busy schedules, seeking tools that preserve the warmth of personal interaction.

Routine Through Play: Children respond more positively to structure when it's delivered through imaginative, character-driven experiences rather than direct instruction.

Define

Framing the problem

Through research and interviews, I found that busy parents often struggle to keep routines consistent and emotionally connected while reducing screen time. They need a warm, playful, and personalized way to maintain those bonds, even when they can’t be physically present.

Developing The Persona

Christina, a nurse and mom of a five-year-old, inspired the persona. She juggles late shifts and family time and wants her child to have a familiar, comforting presence that supports daily routines. She values simple, screen-free solutions that help her stay connected and ease routines without adding complexity.

Mapping the user journey

Looking at the journey, it’s clear that parents like Christina juggle busy schedules while wanting meaningful, screen-free connections with their children. From recording, saving, and broadcasting messages, to creating and sharing AI-generated stories, the process supports comforting routines.

Whether it’s leaving a goodnight message after a late shift or sharing a silly story before school, these tools offer families like Christina’s a way to stay emotionally present, even when physically apart, building connection through consistency, creativity, and care.

Ideate

Paper Wireframes

When I started sketching, my goal was to get many ideas down, so I created a few versions of each page. Then, I highlighted certain elements on each page and combined them into a refined concept. The accompanying figure shows this process for the incorporated Favorite Audios page.

Digital Wireframes

After building the digital wireframe, I tested it with users to evaluate how well two main flows (broadcasting a message, and creating or broadcasting a story) functioned in practice. I observed participants, noting where the structure supported their expectations and where it caused confusion. Feedback showed that much of the functionality felt intuitive, and one participant noted how design choices like color reinforced clarity. Additional feedback helped polish and refine the final prototype.

Prototype

Low-Fidelity Prototype

Once the initial wireframes were complete, I started building out the user flow in Figma to map how parents would interact with the Magical Mates app. I focused on key features like recording a message and broadcasting a story through the plush companion. The goal was to test the functionality of each screen and ensure the navigation made sense, especially for busy parents looking for a quick and intuitive experience. I kept the layout minimal and made sure each action, like starting a recording or scheduling a message, was easy to find and clearly labeled. Since the visuals were still basic at this stage, my priority was making sure the structure supported the emotional and practical goals of the app.

High-Fidelity Prototype

In the high-fidelity prototype, I refined the structure from the low-fidelity version and added visuals that matched the tone of Magical Mates. I used soft colors, friendly icons, and simple illustrations to make the experience feel more inviting. I also updated the microcopy to be clearer and more parent-focused. This version helped me test how the app would feel in real use, whether sending a message or playing a story felt easy, natural, and emotionally supportive.

Test

Sample Improvements from User Feedback

Recently Played Stories

After conducting a few usability studies, I noticed that users wanted an easy way to return to stories they had just listened to. In response, I added a “Recently Played” section to the Storytime page. This change allows users to quickly access their most recent stories without needing to search again, making the experience feel more fluid and user-friendly.

Enhanced Saved Messages Functionality

During testing, it became clear that users wanted more flexibility when interacting with their saved recordings. I updated the Saved Messages page to include the ability to play a message directly from the list, as well as a new “Share” option. These additions made it easier for users to engage with their own content and share it with others, reducing friction and increasing overall usability.

Conclusion

Final Thoughts

Reflecting on the process, I learned a lot more about Figma than I expected, especially how to use overlays and build more advanced interactions in the prototype. I also improved my icon design skills, learning how to create custom icons that matched the tone of the app. Lastly, I also gained a better sense of how to balance creativity with usability, making sure every design choice served a clear purpose. These small details ended up having a big impact on how polished and intuitive the final product felt.

Accessibility Considerations

🌈

High Contrast Colors: Ensured all color choices met WCAG contrast ratio guidelines to support users with visual impairments and enhance readability.

🔠

Readable & Reachable: Used large text and generously sized buttons to improve navigation for users with visual impairments or motor challenges.

🔊

Message Captions: Added captions for all recorded messages, so users with hearing difficulties can still engage with the app’s core features.
